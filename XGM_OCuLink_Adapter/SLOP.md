# Response

Yes, it's possible, and I've designed it: a complete KiCad project is in [XGM_OCuLink_Adapter/](XGM_OCuLink_Adapter/). It is a 33 × 18 mm board that plugs straight into the XGM port and gives you a standard OCuLink 4i port. It is an untested first revision and is not ready to order, because of one part I could not pin down (see "The catch").
## What "redrivers" meant
A redriver is an amplifier that restores a PCIe signal weakened by a long path. That reply assumed the adapter would sit at the end of the ASUS XGM cable, so the signal would cross that cable and then an OCuLink cable. Your idea removes the ASUS cable: the path becomes laptop, about 10 mm of board, one OCuLink cable, dock. That is comparable to the stock setup, so this design is passive. I'd expect PCIe 3.0 to work. 4.0 is plausible on a ROG Flow with a short cable and less likely on an Ally.
## The design
  - **PCIe:** lanes 0–3 and the reference clock pass straight through. Lanes 4–7 are left open, so the link trains at x4.
  - **Handshake:** the laptop won't switch PCIe to the port until a dock answers over the sideband pins. I reused the Lite dock's STM32 on the same pins, so the existing `XG_Mobile_Dock_MCU` firmware should run unmodified.
  - **Power:** a resistor on the port's USB-C CC pin makes the laptop supply 5 V, which feeds the microcontroller through a 3.3 V regulator. This uses up the USB-C half of the port, so a laptop with another USB-C port can still charge, but a first-generation Ally could not.


KiCad's electrical and design-rule checks pass with zero violations, zero unconnected nets, and the board matching the schematic. Every differential pair is length-matched.
## The catch
The male XGM plug is not sold as a part; stores only sell the laptop-side socket. The only source is an ASUS XG Mobile cable (about $129, or one from a dead unit), with the plug desoldered from the small board in the cable head.
Because of that, the plug footprint is an estimate from the repo's photos: pad pitch, pad size, the gap between the USB-C and PCIe sections, and board thickness (0.8 mm) are all guesses. The cut-outs in the board edge are not modelled at all. The board must not be ordered until you have a plug and calipers.
The layout is generated by scripts for this reason. You put the measured values at the top of `gen/make_fp.py`, re-run, and the PCIe routing follows the new numbers (about two minutes).
## Other things to know
  - **Rough low-speed routing:** the microcontroller wiring was done by a small router I wrote after the off-the-shelf autorouter failed. It is correct but untidy, and worth cleaning up by hand once the footprint is final.
  - **Unverified on hardware:**
    - whether the port supplies 5 V early enough at boot for the BIOS check (there is a pad for external 5 V if not);
    - whether the plug stays seated without the original latch;
    - whether your dock powers up on its own.
  - **Other checks before ordering:** the OCuLink footprint is for one specific Amphenol part, and the trace width for 85 Ω is an approximation you should confirm with your fab's calculator.
  - **Linux:** I believe the `asus-nb-wmi` driver exposes `egpu_enable` for switching the eGPU on, but I did not verify that on your kernel.
  - **Autorouter side effect:** my first attempt at running the autorouter may have briefly opened a GUI window on your desktop before I killed it.


Nothing is committed; the new directory is untracked. The [README](XGM_OCuLink_Adapter/README.md) has the full pre-order checklist and the regeneration commands. No extra tools are needed on your side beyond KiCad, numpy and scipy, which you already have.
